What Happens When You Run a Wellness Brand With Zero Employees

May 8, 2026  •  2 min read

← Back to The Journal

People assume Laibar has a team. A marketing person. A customer service rep. Maybe a formulation consultant.

Nope.

Laibar runs with five AI agents and one human (me). No employees. No freelancers. No contractors.

Here's how it actually works.

The Agent Squad

Every AI agent has a role:

  • Titan — Director. Reviews deliverables, assigns tasks, escalates blockers across the full brand.
  • Lux — Creative lead. Maintains brand voice, approves visuals, writes and refines copy.
  • Rex — Research. Monitors clinical studies, verifies ingredient claims, keeps the science honest.
  • Axiom — Growth. Handles pricing, channel strategy, marketplace listings, distribution.
  • Maven — Customer experience. Answers questions, processes returns, designs wellness rituals.

They coordinate through a mission queue — a Kanban board where tasks flow from "inbox" to "done." Each agent checks in on a heartbeat (every 15–30 minutes), picks up work, and delivers.

A Typical Day

6:00 AM — Titan runs the morning review. Any stuck tasks get escalated. The daily brief lands in my inbox.

8:00 AM — I wake up, skim the brief, and approve or adjust priorities. This takes 10 minutes.

Throughout the day — Agents work autonomously. Rex might flag a new study on boswellia. Lux might draft three versions of a product photo caption. Maven might handle a shipping question.

11:00 PM — Nightly build wraps up whatever was started. I review anything needing final approval before it ships.

I don't manage tasks. I manage direction.

Why This Works

1. AI doesn't forget. Every agent has access to the same memory files — brand guidelines, customer FAQs, ingredient specs. They don't need to be re-briefed. They don't lose context between sessions.

2. AI doesn't get tired. Agents check the queue on a schedule. They don't burn out. They don't take mental health days.

3. AI is brutally consistent. Lux doesn't have "off days" where the copy is sloppy. Rex doesn't skip a study because it looked boring. The quality floor is higher than any human team I've managed.

What Still Requires a Human

Let's be clear: I'm not fully automated out of my own business.

  • Strategy — Which products to launch, which channels to prioritize, when to pivot.
  • Taste — Final approval on creative, tone, anything customer-facing.
  • Relationships — Suppliers, partners, anyone who wants to talk to "the founder."

The agents handle execution. I handle judgment.

The Economics

Traditional supplement brand: 10–15 people minimum. Salaries, benefits, management overhead.

Laibar: API costs (a few hundred dollars/month) plus my time.

That delta goes straight into ingredients. Better sourcing. More rigorous testing. No "cost optimization" that cuts corners.
